Frostfret: a guitar you play from MIDI, for demos and pre-production

Frostfret is a VST3 instrument that plays electric, steel-string and nylon guitar and electric bass from MIDI, with modelled strings instead of samples. It belongs to the Code Chaos Black Metal Suite by Tim Borchert, who masters at Polished. It is made for the moment a song exists only in someone's head or in a MIDI file: the riff can be heard as a guitar part with tremolo picking, palm mutes and a double-tracked wall before anyone picks up a real guitar.

What Frostfret sounds like, honestly

Frostfret is a model. Every note is a simulated string that is picked, rings, beats against itself and decays; there are no recorded guitar notes inside. No synthesised guitar sounds exactly like a recorded one, and Frostfret does not either.

  • Closest to a recording: distorted guitars (crunch and high gain) and the steel-string and nylon acoustics. The electric bass is close as well.
  • Most clearly synthetic: the clean electric guitar. You hear it in the first half of the clean/crunch clip above.
  • Two voicings: Natural is the current model, Classic (v1) keeps the darker sound of version 1. The Air knob makes strings and mic brighter or softer.

Code Chaos Audio checks every sound type with an automated listening model against real recordings, and the result above is what that check says. Listen to the three clips or load the free demo before you buy.

A guitar demo without the guitarist

Many songs start with one person who has the riff but not the time, the tone or the guitar player at hand. With Frostfret, the riff becomes a guitar part the band can rehearse to:

  1. Write the part as MIDI. Hold a note and Frostfret picks it as tremolo in 1/16, 1/16 triplets or 1/32, synced to your DAW tempo, with alternating down- and upstrokes. One key plays a power chord. Tunings go from E standard down to B standard, including Drop D and Drop C.
  2. Mark the mutes. Palm mute follows a velocity zone, the mod wheel or a keyswitch, and it is continuous. Gallops and chugs sound muted without a second track.
  3. Send the demo, keep the MIDI. When the real guitars are recorded later, the MIDI part shows the exact riff and tempo, and the Frostfret track serves as a guide.

For the final record, a real performance through Gravesaw or a real amp usually still sounds better. For writing, pre-production and rough mixes, Frostfret saves the session.

Double tracking that survives mono

Turn on Double Track and Frostfret plays the same MIDI part as two independent takes, hard left and hard right, each with its own timing and picking. That is how a real rhythm wall is recorded, and it is why the wall holds up when a phone or a club system sums it to mono.

Below 160 Hz, Frostfret keeps the output mono (with Double Track up to about 250 Hz), so the low end cannot cancel. Every preset is tested for mono compatibility. In the black metal clip on this page, the two sides correlate at 0.97 below 200 Hz, and folding the whole mix to mono costs 1.7 dB. A copied and delayed single take, the usual shortcut, often loses much more and sounds hollow. A master cannot repair that afterwards, which is why the audit at Polished checks phase and mono before every master.

DI Out into Gravesaw, or the amp built in

Frostfret contains the amp of Gravesaw in four modes (Clean, Crunch, Overdrive, High-Gain) with the same 19 synthesised cabs. For a quick demo that is enough. For a mix you may want more control:

  • Switch the amp to DI Out. Frostfret then sends a clean DI signal, and you put Gravesaw or any other amp plugin after it, exactly as with a recorded DI guitar.
  • Print the DI as its own track. Amp decisions stay open until the mix, and you can re-amp the part without playing the MIDI again.
  • Set the amp gate before it matters. The noise between riffs gets louder in mastering, so gate it in the mix, not in the master.

Export the finished mix with 3 to 6 dB of headroom and no limiter on the master bus. The black metal mastering page explains what happens after that.

Frostfret FAQ

Does Frostfret sound like a real guitar?
Close, depending on the sound. Distorted and acoustic guitars come closest to a recording, the clean electric guitar sounds the most synthetic. Frostfret is modelled and uses no samples. Listen to the three demo clips on this page or try the free demo.
Can Frostfret replace the guitarist on our album?
For demos, songwriting and pre-production it can stand in. For a release, recorded guitars usually still sound better, and the Frostfret MIDI part then serves as a guide for the real takes.
Can I use my own amp plugin?
Yes. Set the amp to DI Out; Frostfret then sends a clean DI signal, and you put Gravesaw or any other amp after it.
Does it run on a Mac?
Yes, on Apple Silicon Macs (arm64) as VST3 and Standalone. There is no Intel build and no AU version, so Logic Pro is not supported. The macOS build is not signed or notarized: macOS blocks it at first, and you allow it under System Settings → Privacy & Security.
Is there a free trial?
Yes. The Frostfret demo on Gumroad is the full instrument with all 30 presets; it plays a short noise burst every 45 seconds.
