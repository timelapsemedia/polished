Hoarfell: drums, bass and a choir pad for demos and pre-production

Hoarfell is a VST3 instrument with three engines: a drum kit on the General MIDI map that keeps up with blast beats, a modelled electric bass, and a choir, organ, strings, bell and keys section with a long hall. Like its sister plugin Frostfret it is modelled, with no samples inside, and it comes from Tim Borchert, who masters at Polished. It is the quickest way to turn a riff and a drum MIDI file into a song the whole band can hear.

Drums for demos and pre-production

Hoarfell reads the General MIDI drum map: kick on 36, snare on 38, hats on 42, 44 and 46, toms, two crashes, two rides, bell and china. A groove programmed in any DAW, played on an e-kit or taken from the 30 MIDI grooves of the Black Metal Drum Kit plays right away.

  • One engine per instance. Drums, bass and atmosphere each get their own track. That keeps the demo mixable and gives you clean stems if you want to send stems to mastering.
  • Close mics, overheads and room per kit, with each drum placed in the overhead pair. Six kits from studio to lo-fi rehearsal tape.
  • Keep the tempo map. When the real drums are recorded later, the drummer can play to the same tempo and arrangement the band rehearsed with.

Hoarfell is built for writing, demos and pre-production. For a release, recorded drums or a dedicated drum production are usually the better choice, and that is how the plugin is meant: as the drummer who is always available while the songs take shape.

Built for blast beats

Fast sixteenths are where many drum plugins start to click or smear. In Hoarfell a re-triggered drum fades its previous hit over 3 ms, so 250 BPM sixteenths stay click-free in the automated tests. Round Robin varies every hit, and velocity changes contact time and brightness, so a blast does not sound like the same sample fired 16 times per bar.

One thing to set in the mix, not in the master: the Cymbals control balances crashes, rides, china and bell against kick, snare, toms and hats. A cymbal wash that covers the guitars is one of the most common findings in black metal premasters, and mastering EQ cannot lower the cymbals without dulling everything else.

The choir belongs behind the mix

The atmosphere engine has a modelled choir of up to eight singers per note, each with its own voice, pitch, vibrato and entry. It sings vowels only (Ah, Oh, Uh), no words. On sustained chords, Ah and Oh come close to a recorded choir; the low Uh drone less so. Use it as a pad behind guitars and drums, not as a lead choir.

Long pads and halls fill every gap in a mix, and a limiter in mastering brings quiet parts up. If the choir already sounds present in the rough mix, it will sound louder after mastering. Keep it a little lower than feels right, as in the full mix above, and check the song in mono: below 160 Hz, Hoarfell keeps its output mono so the low end cannot cancel. The section also has a drawbar organ, a strings pad, a cold bell, celesta and lo-fi keys.

Hoarfell FAQ

Can I use my own drum MIDI with Hoarfell?
Yes. Hoarfell uses the General MIDI drum map, so grooves from your DAW, an e-kit or the Black Metal Drum Kit play without remapping.
Does Hoarfell replace a drummer or a drum library for the album?
It is made for demos and pre-production. For a release, recorded drums or a dedicated drum production usually remain the better choice.
Can the choir sing lyrics?
No. It sings the vowels Ah, Oh and Uh, so it works as a choir pad behind the mix, not as a lead choir.
Do I need Frostfret as well?
No. Hoarfell runs on its own. The guitars are in Frostfret; both together are part of the Black Metal Suite Complete bundle.
Does it run on a Mac?
Yes, on Apple Silicon Macs (arm64) as VST3 and Standalone. There is no Intel build and no AU version, so Logic Pro is not supported. The macOS build is not signed or notarized, so you allow it once under System Settings → Privacy & Security.
Is there a free trial?
Yes. The Hoarfell demo on Gumroad is the full instrument with all 20 presets; it plays a short noise burst every 45 seconds.
