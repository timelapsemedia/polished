Crucible: 3-band saturation for bass, drums and buses

Crucible splits your signal into low, mid and high and saturates each band on its own, with five types per band: Hard Clip, Wavefolder, Tube, Diode and Tape. Tim Borchert built it under Code Chaos Audio for psytrance production. The same split solves a classic metal problem: a bass that needs grind in the mids while the lowest octave stays clean and solid.

Split-band saturation for a metal bass

Distorting a whole bass signal is the quickest way to lose the low end: the lowest notes turn into fuzz, and the mix gets muddy under the guitars. With three bands you can treat each register for what it has to do.

  • Low band: little or no drive. Keep it clean and steady, so kick and bass still work together. Check it with the free Kick/Bass Scope.
  • Mid band: this is where the grind lives that cuts through rhythm guitars on small speakers. Diode or Hard Clip add bite, Tube is rounder.
  • High band: Tape softens string noise and fret clank, more drive brings it forward.

The two crossovers are adjustable, and every band has its own Drive, Mix, Gain, Mute and Solo. For heavy settings, raise the oversampling (Off, 2x, 4x or 8x) to keep aliasing down.

Saturation on the mix bus before mastering?

Saturation lowers the difference between peaks and average level. Once it is printed into the mix, it cannot be undone in mastering, and an over-squashed premaster leaves the master with less room to work. If you like Crucible on your mix bus, send two versions to mastering: one with it and one without, and say which one you prefer. The audit at Polished flags over-limited and over-saturated premasters before anything is processed.

Crucible FAQ

Does Crucible run on a Mac?
Not at the moment. Crucible is a Windows 10/11 plugin (VST3 and Standalone).
Is there a free trial?
Yes. The Crucible demo on Gumroad is the complete plugin with every band, every saturation type and oversampling up to 8x; it plays a short burst of quiet noise every 45 seconds.
Should I put Crucible on my master bus before sending the mix to mastering?
Only if it is part of the sound you want. Send a second version without it as well, so the mastering engineer can choose. Saturation that is printed into the mix cannot be removed later.
