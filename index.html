Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know.

What exactly is the audio audit?
An audio audit is a full technical and perceptual analysis of your mix, carried out before any mastering decision is made. Before every mastering session I run this analysis across your track — frequency spectrum, dynamics, loudness profile, stereo image, phase coherence, mono compatibility, and a sonic assessment in genre context, meaning what a competitive Black Metal or Death Metal master needs to sound like versus a Doom or Gothic one. You get a written analysis report with concretely identified weaknesses — for example a resonant frequency buildup, an overly narrow stereo image, or a loudness level that won't translate to streaming platforms — and a documented mastering strategy explaining exactly which moves will be made and why. The audit is included in every mastering package at no extra cost, and it's the reason mastering here starts with data instead of guesswork.
What does professional audio mastering cost?
Stereo mastering starts at €79 per track, which includes the full audio audit — you're never charged separately for the analysis step. Stem mastering, which gives more precise control by working with grouped stems instead of a single stereo file, starts at €129 per track. For a full release, the EP package covers up to 5 tracks for €349 (€46 less than five singles), and the Album package covers up to 10 tracks for €629 (€161 less than ten single masters), both including per-track audits plus a consistency pass across the whole release. All prices are final prices including 19% German VAT. There's no subscription, no recurring fee, and no hidden add-on costs — the price you see is the price you pay, and every package already includes unlimited revisions until the master is right.
Do you offer label rates or bundles?
Yes. Labels with a release schedule (from 4 releases per year) get fixed partner rates: €63 per stereo single, €103 per stem single, €279 per EP and €499 per album. Artists releasing several singles can book a Single Series: 3 singles at €73 each, mastered as they come out over up to 12 months. The Stem EP covers up to 5 tracks from stems for €549. You can pay up front or on invoice after delivery, whichever you prefer. All prices are final prices including 19% German VAT, and the audit and unlimited revisions are always included.
How long does mastering take?
Standard delivery for a single track is 48–72 hours, and that already includes the full audio audit — you're not waiting longer because the audit happens first. EPs, which involve consistency-checking across up to 5 tracks, typically take 5–7 days. Full albums, covering up to 10 tracks with a whole-release loudness and tonal consistency pass, typically take 7–14 days. If you need something faster, rush delivery in 24 hours is available on request. If a release date is locked in — a label deadline, a scheduled release — say so upfront and the schedule will be planned around it rather than around a generic queue.
What's the difference between stereo and stem mastering?
Stereo mastering processes your finished stereo mixdown — a single file, a single stereo track, exactly as you bounced it. Stem mastering works differently: instead of one file, you send grouped stems, for example drums, bass, guitars and vocals as separate stereo files, and each group can be treated individually before everything is combined into the final master. That separation gives significantly more control — a guitar wall that's masking the vocals, or a kick that needs more headroom than the rest of the mix, can be addressed directly instead of compromised around. The audio audit will tell you which approach actually makes sense for your specific mix.
What genres do you master?
Polished is fully specialized in Metal & Gothic — no pop, no hip-hop, no generic all-genre template. Black Metal mastering means preserving raw, tremolo-picked atmosphere and cold high end without losing low-end control. Death Metal mastering means giving palm-mutes real weight and keeping blast beats readable instead of collapsing into noise. Doom and Gothic mastering means protecting dynamic range and space for slow builds while keeping the low end thick without swallowing the rest of the mix. Metalcore and Djent mastering means precise, controlled low end for syncopated riffs and clarity through breakdowns. That focus means every audio audit and every reference comparison is built specifically around what matters in this scene, not a generic template borrowed from pop or hip-hop production.
What file formats do you need?
Send 24-bit or 32-bit WAV or AIFF files at your session's original sample rate — 44.1, 48, 88.2 or 96 kHz all work. Leave 3–6 dB of headroom on your mixdown, and avoid putting a limiter or compressor on the master bus before sending, since that removes the dynamic range needed for accurate mastering decisions. The same applies to loudness maximizing in the mix itself — a track that's already been pushed to streaming loudness before mastering limits what the audit and the mastering process can actually improve. If you're unsure whether your mix meets these requirements, send it anyway; the audit will flag anything that needs attention before mastering begins.
What happens if I'm not happy with the master?
You get unlimited revisions on every package — no extra cost, no time limit, no argument about whether a revision is reasonable. If a master doesn't fit, say so specifically: too harsh in the highs, not enough low-end weight for the genre, vocals sitting too far back, whatever it is. I'll listen closely, ask what's off compared to your reference expectations, and rework the master until it matches your vision rather than my interpretation of it. This applies to every package tier equally, from a single €79 track to a full album.
Is Polished an AI mastering service like LANDR?
No. Every track sent to Polished is mastered personally, and more importantly, every track is individually analyzed first through the audio audit. AI mastering services are cheap because they're generic: an algorithm can apply loudness and EQ curves, but it can't produce a written audit explaining what's actually wrong with your specific mix, can't understand what you're trying to achieve creatively, and can't develop a mastering strategy tailored to Black Metal, Death Metal, Doom or Gothic conventions specifically. Polished is the analytical, personal alternative to that — data-driven rather than algorithmic, at a fair price.
Do you also offer mixing?
Polished specializes in mastering — not mixing — and that specialization is intentional rather than a limitation. Mixing and mastering require different skill sets and different listening approaches, and trying to offer both often means neither is done as well as it could be. If the audio audit reveals that your mix itself needs work before mastering can do its job, that will be communicated honestly upfront, along with a recommendation for a mixing engineer from within my network rather than attempting a mastering fix that won't actually solve the underlying problem.
Where is the Polished studio located?
Polished is an online mastering studio based in Lower Saxony, Germany, operating under Heart Warrior UG in Kirchlinteln. There's no physical studio visit involved in the process — audio files are exchanged entirely online through secure transfer services like WeTransfer, Dropbox or Google Drive, and the audit report and final masters are delivered the same way. That remote setup means location isn't a barrier: clients are served across Germany, Austria and Switzerland, as well as internationally, with all communication handled by email or Instagram DM regardless of time zone.
How loud should a metal master be?
There's no single correct number. Spotify, YouTube and Tidal normalize playback to around -14 LUFS integrated and Apple Music to around -16 LUFS, so a louder master is simply turned down on those platforms. Many commercial metal releases still land somewhere around -10 to -7 LUFS integrated because the genre's density and saturation are part of the sound. The real question is how loud your specific mix can get before blast beats, cymbals and low end start to collapse. The audio audit measures exactly that, and the loudness target is set per track and genre rather than pushed to the maximum by default. For louder masters, true peak is kept at or below -1 dBTP (Spotify recommends -2 dBTP above -14 LUFS) so lossy streaming encodes don't distort. More detail in the metal mastering loudness guide.
Can you master for vinyl and Bandcamp?
Yes. Bandcamp does not normalize loudness, so the Bandcamp master is the version listeners hear exactly as delivered, and it's planned accordingly. A separate vinyl-ready master is available on request for album projects: vinyl cutting benefits from more dynamic range, controlled sibilance and a mono-compatible low end, which the audit's phase and stereo analysis already checks. Mention the formats you plan to release on when you get in touch, and the deliverables will be set up for each of them.
How does invoicing work?
No upfront payment is required: you can pay on invoice after delivery, or up front if you prefer. Every invoice includes a full VAT statement as required under German business law. Payment can be made by bank transfer or PayPal, with other arrangements available on request for labels or agencies with specific procurement requirements. B2B terms are available for labels and agencies working across multiple releases, and regular clients — bands or labels sending tracks on an ongoing basis — can arrange a framework agreement with preferred pricing rather than negotiating each project individually.
