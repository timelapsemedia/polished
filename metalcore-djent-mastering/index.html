What metalcore and djent need from a master

Modern metalcore and djent are produced tight, loud and polished, often with programmed drums, extended-range guitars and synth layers. Listeners compare your release directly to big-budget productions. The master has to deliver that modern loudness and polish while keeping the low end controlled when 7- and 8-string guitars, bass and kick all live below 100 Hz.

/ 01

Metalcore & post-hardcore

Big choruses, clean vocals and heavy breakdowns in one song. The master keeps both halves powerful without the chorus getting lost.

/ 02

Djent & prog metal

Syncopated low-end riffs need definition. The master keeps every palm-muted chug distinct instead of one continuous rumble.

/ 03

Deathcore

Extreme low end and extreme vocals. The audit watches headroom carefully so drops hit without distortion.

/ 04

Electronic-hybrid

Synths, sub drops and samples on top of the band. The master balances sub energy so it doesn't steal headroom from the guitars.

What the audit typically finds in metalcore and djent mixes

  • Low-end collisions below 100 Hz between extended-range guitars, bass, kick and sub drops.
  • Breakdowns that get quieter after limiting because the low end eats all the headroom.
  • Clean vocals too far back in choruses, or screams too harsh in the 3–5 kHz range.
  • Sub drops and 808s that overload the limiter and cause pumping.
  • Phase issues from layered guitar DIs, re-amps and stereo widening that weaken the mix in mono.

Each finding is documented with measured values and a planned fix, including an honest note if something should be changed in the mix first.

How a metalcore or djent master comes together

  1. Premaster in: 24/32-bit WAV or AIFF at session sample rate, 3–6 dB headroom, no master-bus limiter, plus one or two reference tracks.
  2. Written audio audit: frequency spectrum, dynamics and loudness, stereo image and phase, and a sonic assessment against your subgenre and references.
  3. Mastering plan: every move is listed with the reason behind it before anything is processed.
  4. Master + exports: 24-bit WAV, 16-bit AIFF, 320 kbps MP3, platform-specific versions for streaming and Bandcamp.
  5. Revisions: unlimited, until it matches what you hear in your head.

Metalcore is one of the loudest-mastered metal styles. That's achievable, but the cleanest results come from a mix with headroom and no master-bus limiter. See the metal mastering loudness guide for targets and true-peak limits.

Metalcore & Djent Mastering FAQ

Can you get my metalcore master as loud as major releases?
Usually close, if the mix allows it. Modern metalcore is often mastered somewhere around -8 to -6 LUFS integrated. Whether your mix can get there cleanly depends on how controlled the low end is and how much headroom the premaster has. The audit measures that first and tells you honestly what loudness is possible without distortion or pumping.
How do you handle 7- and 8-string low end?
The audit looks at where guitar, bass and kick energy overlap below 100 Hz and checks phase between them. The master then controls that region so palm-muted riffs stay distinct. When the collision is deep in the mix, stem mastering (€129 per track, up to 6 stems) allows guitars and bass to be shaped separately.
Do breakdowns and sub drops get special attention?
Yes. Breakdowns are often where masters fail: the low end eats the limiter's headroom and the heaviest moment ends up feeling smaller. The audit flags those sections, and the master is checked specifically on breakdowns and drops, not only on the loudest chorus.
Do you work with programmed drums and amp sims?
Yes. Programmed drums, amp simulators and in-the-box productions are standard in metalcore and djent and are mastered exactly like tracked material. The audit checks sample-based kicks and snares for clicks or harshness after limiting.
